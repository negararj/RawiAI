# Al Zubarah

Picture a walled town rising straight out of the flat coastal desert, its streets, courtyard houses, mosques, and market laid out exactly as they were left - because almost no one ever came back to build over them.

That town is Al Zubarah, founded in the 18th century by merchants who arrived from Kuwait to trade along Qatar's northwest coast. Within a few decades it grew into one of the largest and wealthiest towns in the Gulf, its fortunes built on pearling and long-distance trade stretching from India to East Africa.

Al Zubarah's success made it a target. The town was destroyed in 1811, rebuilt, and later abandoned through the 19th and early 20th centuries as the pearling industry it depended on collapsed - undercut by the arrival of cultured pearls from Japan. By the 1940s, it was empty.

That abandonment turned out to be a gift to archaeologists. Because no modern city was ever built on top of it, Al Zubarah survives as one of the most complete examples of a fortified Gulf trading town anywhere in the region - its town walls, canal, harbor, and building foundations still traceable in the sand.

In 2013, Al Zubarah became Qatar's first and only UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognized as the best-preserved example of the pearling towns that once defined the entire Gulf coast.

**Walk its empty streets, and you're tracing the outline of a town that pearl divers, traders, and their families once called home.**
