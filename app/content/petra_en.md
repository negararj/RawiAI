# Petra

Imagine walking through a narrow canyon, its rose-colored walls rising more than sixty meters on either side, the sky reduced to a thin ribbon of blue above you.

This is the Siq, the winding gorge that has served as Petra's main entrance for over two thousand years. Follow it far enough, and the walls suddenly part to reveal Al-Khazneh - the Treasury - its columned facade carved directly into the sandstone cliff.

Petra was the capital of the Nabataeans, a trading people who grew wealthy controlling the caravan routes that carried incense, spices, and silk across the ancient world. They didn't just carve tombs and temples into the rock - they mastered the desert itself, channeling flash floods through hidden cisterns and dams to keep their city alive through the driest seasons.

Beyond the Treasury lies a city built almost entirely from stone: a Roman-style theatre cut into the hillside, royal tombs with weathered facades, and further still, a climb of hundreds of steps leading to the Monastery, Ad Deir, even larger than the Treasury and far quieter.

Petra was lost to much of the outside world for centuries, known only to local Bedouin, until a Swiss explorer rediscovered it in 1812. Today it stands as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New7Wonders of the World.

**Walk through the Siq, and you are walking the same path traders and travelers have followed for over two millennia.**
