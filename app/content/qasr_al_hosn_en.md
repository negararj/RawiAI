# Qasr Al Hosn

Long before Abu Dhabi grew into a skyline of glass and steel, a small watchtower stood on this same ground, built to guard the only freshwater well on the island.

Over time, that watchtower grew into Qasr Al Hosn — a coral-and-limestone fort that became the seat of the Al Nahyan ruling family and, for generations, the beating heart of government in Abu Dhabi.

Look closely at its thick, whitewashed walls. They were built to survive heat, wind, and time, using materials pulled straight from the sea and the desert around them: coral stone, gypsum, and palm wood.

For decades, decisions that shaped the emirate were made within these walls. Today, Qasr Al Hosn has been carefully restored, standing not as a relic behind glass, but as the living memory of how a small settlement around a well became a capital city.

**You are standing at the very point where Abu Dhabi began.**
